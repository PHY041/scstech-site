How the belt works

One manure line, three belt sections

In a stacked layer house, every tier has its own longitudinal belt under the cage. At the end of the row, a lateral belt collects manure from all tiers and passes it to an inclined belt that carries it out of the house. SCS supplies belts for all three sections, so the hand-offs are planned together.

4 to 12 tiers. Each with its own belt.

China’s 2023 technical guidance for stacked layer houses calls for belt-type manure removal with a belt under every tier, so manure never reaches the floor.

Industry practice: MARA, Technical Guidance for Vertical Layer Production (2023).

Why SCS

Why buyers specify with SCS

  1. 01

    Fit first.

    There is no universal belt width — it follows the cage. We start from your cage drawing or measured cage floor, tiers and row length.

  2. 02

    The right joint for the material.

    PP is non-polar, so conventional RF (high-frequency) welding is generally unsuitable; PP belts are joined by ultrasonic or hot-plate welding. We make our own ultrasonic belt welder.

  3. 03

    The whole line from one supplier.

    In-cage, lateral and inclined belts plus egg belts — one specification sheet, one shipment.

  4. 04

    Engineering answers, not slogans.

    Our guides cite China’s 2023 MARA technical guidance and current industry standards, so you can check every recommendation.

The joint follows the material.

PP belts are joined by ultrasonic or hot-plate welding. PVC suits RF welding. We make our own ultrasonic belt welder for PP.

How many square metres of belt do you need?

A closed-loop belt runs the row twice — once carrying manure, once returning. Enter belt width, row length, tiers and rows to get an estimate in m² and linear metres, then send it to us for a checked quotation.
